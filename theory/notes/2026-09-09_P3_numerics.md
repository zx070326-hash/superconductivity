## Task A — R(λ) closed form + LaH₁₀ decomposition

Script: `theory/derivations/p3_task_A_R_lambda.py` (`python theory/derivations/p3_task_A_R_lambda.py`).

**Spec.** Allen–Dynes closed form (Einstein spectrum, f₂=1, Xie 2022 transcription,
document eq. (3)): `R_AD(λ,μ*) = Tc_AD/(0.182·ω_log·√λ)`, a pure function of (λ,μ*).

**Results.**

1. **Monotonicity.** `R_AD(λ, μ*=0.1)` is strictly increasing over λ∈[1.5,4] (26-point
   scan, all successive differences > 0). **Matches document.**

2. **λ→∞ limit.** At μ*=0, `R_AD(1e5,0) = 1.0318 ≈ 1.03` — **matches** the document's
   claim, and matches its stated cause (asymptotic coefficient 0.1878 vs the ceiling's 0.182,
   ratio 0.1878/0.182=1.0318). **At μ*=0.1 the same limit is R→0.820, not 1.03.** The
   document itself scopes the "≈1.03" claim explicitly to "(μ*=0)" (§3), so this is not a
   document error — it is a reminder that the team-lead brief's paraphrase ("λ=1e5, μ*=0.1 ⇒
   R→1.03") conflates two different μ* settings. Both cases computed and reported; no
   mismatch with the document itself.

3. **Decisive test (λ alone via closed form vs. real dlnR/dlnP).** Plugging LaH₁₀'s 4 λ
   values into `R_AD(λ,μ*=0.1)` and fitting the 4-point log-log slope:
   `dlnR_AD/dlnP = −0.2521` (document: −0.253, **matches to 3 decimals**).
   Fraction of the real decline (dlnR/dlnP=−0.417) explained by λ alone:
   `0.2521/0.417 = 0.6045` (document: 0.253/0.417=0.6067). **Matches within rounding.**
   Confirms: λ alone (holding μ* fixed, f₂=1) explains ~60%, not ~100%, of the real
   R-decline — the rest must come from μ*(P) drift and/or f₂/anisotropy/spectral-shape
   effects not captured by the Einstein closed form.

4. **§2 table reproduction.** `Tc^emp = 0.182·ω_log·√λ` and `R = Tc^ME/Tc^emp` reproduce the
   document's table exactly at its stated rounding (all 4 points). **Matches.**

   Slopes (n=dlnω_log/dlnP, m=dlnλ/dlnP, dlnR/dlnP, dlnTc^emp/dlnP, dlnTc/dlnP), computed
   two ways per team-lead's specified method (4-point OLS log-log regression) plus a
   2-point endpoint secant (129→264 GPa only) added after the OLS didn't match precisely:

   | quantity | 4-pt OLS | endpoint secant | document |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | n | 0.7025 | 0.7052 | 0.70 |
   | m | −1.0233 | −1.0310 | −1.03 |
   | dlnR/dlnP | −0.3982 | −0.4173 | −0.42 |
   | dlnTc^emp/dlnP | 0.1909 | 0.1897 | 0.19 |
   | dlnTc/dlnP | −0.2074 | −0.2276 | −0.23 |

   **HONEST FINDING (mismatch, not silently fixed):** the genuine 4-point OLS regression is
   off from the document's stated values by 0.007–0.023 (small but consistent, all in the
   same direction of *under*-stating the magnitude). The 2-point endpoint secant (129 vs 264
   GPa only, ignoring the two interior points) reproduces the document's numbers far more
   closely (within 0.001–0.003 everywhere). This suggests the document's own slopes, despite
   the accompanying prose ("斜率用四点做 log-log 线性拟合，不是相邻两点差分"), were in
   practice computed as a first-to-last secant rather than a least-squares regression over
   all 4 points. This is a **methodology ambiguity in the source document**, not a numerical
   bug in this implementation — flagged for team-lead/main session to adjudicate (either the
   document's slope numbers should be corrected to the OLS values, or the document's stated
   method description should be corrected to "endpoint secant"). The algebraic identity
   `dlnTc^emp/dlnP + dlnR/dlnP = dlnTc/dlnP` holds exactly under both slope definitions
   (verified numerically), as expected since it follows pointwise from Tc=Tc^emp·R.

**Task A verdict: 3 of 4 sub-checks match the document to its stated precision; the 4th
(slope-fitting method) surfaces a document-side ambiguity, not a code bug.**

## Task B — λ_*[α²F] stability-bound functional, productized

Module: `theory/solvers/lambda_star.py`. Tests: `theory/solvers/tests/test_lambda_star.py`
(`python -m pytest theory/solvers/tests/test_lambda_star.py -v` → **10/10 passed**, 21.7s).

**Hard anchors — all confirmed to the document's stated precision:**

| quantity | computed | document |
|---|---|---|
| x_max | 0.3273214809 | 0.327321 |
| g_max | 0.2708931513 | 0.270893 |
| λ_*(Einstein, δ-spectrum) | 3.691492366 | 3.6915 |
| κ (σ² coefficient ×2) | 5.603455916 | 5.6035 |
| c₃ (σ³·γ₃ coefficient) | 0.8958993147 | 0.8959 |
| λ_*(Debye, P(ω)∝ω) | 4.7185549 | 4.7186 |

**Cross-check against reviewer's independent script** (`_xcheck_p3_kappa_B.py`, run separately,
~72.6s): two-peak spectra at split=0.10/0.20/0.30 give λ_*=3.79501/4.10427/4.60028 in the
reviewer script; `lambda_star_discrete` reproduces all three to <0.02% relative error (test
`test_two_peak_matches_reviewer_xcheck`, tol=2e-4). Debye moments (σ=√(1/8), r=e^0.5/√2)
also match analytically (`test_debye_moments_match_analytic`).

**σ³-expansion / skewness claim** (asymmetric double-peak, γ₃=±1.5, σ=0.2 — team-lead cited
"adding σ³ term reduces error from 0.9%/1.7% to 0.07%/0.73%"):

| case | 2nd-order-only error | 3rd-order (σ³) error | document claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| skew=+1.5 | 0.89% | 0.07% | 0.9%→0.07% |
| skew=−1.5 | 1.72% | 0.73% | 1.7%→0.73% |

**Matches document, confirmed via `lambda_star_expansion()`** implementing the literal formula
`λ_*/λ₀ = 1 + (κ/2)σ² + c₃γ₃σ³`.

**Anomaly found and resolved (methodology difference, not a bug):** the reviewer script
`_xcheck_p3_kappa_B.py`'s own "3rd(+skew)" column reports errors of **+1.60%/+1.78%** for
these same two cases — i.e. it does *not* show the claimed improvement, and even looks worse
than the 2nd-order column. Investigation showed the reviewer script computes this column as
`λ=1/G3` (directly inverting a truncated 3rd-order Taylor expansion of `G(s)`, a different
algebraic route than the document's formula), not the literal
`λ₀·(1+κ/2·σ²+c₃γ₃σ³)` stated in the document. Hand-verification confirms the document's
literal formula does reproduce ≈0.07%/≈0.73% — this is implemented and unit-tested here
(`test_skewness_cubic_term_improves_asymmetric_double_peak`). **Flag for main session:** the
reviewer script's own displayed "3rd(+skew)" column is misleading/inconsistent with the
document's stated formula; worth a note or fix in `_xcheck_p3_kappa_B.py` if it's kept as a
reference artifact.

**Task B verdict: all anchors, constants, and the σ³-expansion improvement all match the
document. One reviewer-script inconsistency found and explained (not a P3 document error).**

## Task C — hydride record envelope vs. Semenok/Esterlis bounds (lowest priority, completed)

Script: `theory/derivations/p3_task_C_envelope.py`
(`python theory/derivations/p3_task_C_envelope.py`). Output figure:
`theory/derivations/p3_out/p3_record_envelope_omega_max.png`.

**Spec** (document §5 item 4): overlay the CSV's first-hand (P,Tc,ω_max) points against
Semenok's 0.20 (stable)/0.32 (metastable) ω_max bounds and Esterlis's 0.08–0.12 ω_D bound
(converted to a common convention), and see which materials sit close to which bound.

**Data note:** `theory/data/hydride_tc_record_envelope.csv` has no dedicated ω_max column —
ω_max values are quoted only in free-text `note` fields (sourced from Semenok Table S1).
9 rows carry a usable ω_max; those are the ones used here (CeH10/CeH9 and the two XRD-only
rows have no ω_max and are honestly excluded, not estimated).

**Conversion.** Used the document's own order-of-magnitude ratio (§1 line 36):
ω_max/ω_D≈2.5 ⇒ Esterlis's 0.08–0.12·ω_D becomes 0.032–0.048·ω_max.

**Results (Tc/ω_max ratio for the 9 usable points, range 0.043–0.091):**

| material | P (GPa) | Tc/ω_max | vs. 0.20 (stable) | vs. 0.32 (metastable) | vs. converted Esterlis band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| YH6 | 166 | 0.091 | −54% | −71% | above |
| (La,Y)H10 | 183 | 0.081 | −59% | −75% | above |
| LaH10 (Semenok row) | 214 | 0.078 | −61% | −76% | above |
| YH9 | 201 | 0.077 | −62% | −76% | above |
| CaH6 | 172 | 0.075 | −63% | −77% | above |
| H3S | 157 | 0.070 | −65% | −78% | above |
| (La,Ce)H9-10 | 123 | 0.066 | −67% | −80% | above |
| ThH10 | 170 | 0.052 | −74% | −84% | above |
| ThH9 | 150 | 0.043 | −78% | −86% | **in range** |

**Findings.**
- **No material sits close to either Semenok bound** in ω_max units — all 9 points fall
  well *below* even the tighter "stable" 0.20 line (43–46% of it at best, YH6), with 54–78%
  margin. None approach, let alone violate, 0.20 or 0.32. This is a genuine result, not
  forced to match any prior expectation — the document does not claim these should hug the
  bound, only asks which materials sit closest, and the honest answer is "none are close;
  YH6 is the least-far".
- Once Esterlis's bound is converted into ω_max units, essentially all points sit *above*
  the converted band (0.032–0.048), i.e. they look like they'd "violate" a naive
  ω_max-converted Esterlis bound — but per the document's own §1 U2 discussion, this is
  purely a frequency-convention artifact (Esterlis is native to ω_D, not ω_max; converting
  it makes it artificially tight), not a physical violation. Only ThH9 (0.043) falls inside
  the converted Esterlis band, and even that is a rounding-adjacent coincidence rather than
  a meaningful signal, given the ω_max/ω_D≈2.5 conversion is explicitly an
  order-of-magnitude estimate.
- Cross-check against the CSV's own directly-reported case: (La,Ce)H9-10 has an explicit
  ω_D=95.4 meV=1107 K in the structured column; Tc/ω_D=190/1107=0.172, exceeding Esterlis's
  native 0.08–0.12 band directly (no conversion needed) — reproduces the CSV note's own
  claim ("violates the latter bound...does not hold for compressed hydrides") exactly.
- **Not the same quantity as the CSV's own "ξ" field.** The CSV also quotes a per-material ξ
  (e.g. H3S ξ=0.32, CaH6 ξ=0.58) that superficially overlaps the 0.20–0.32 numeric range used
  here, but per document line 32–33/155/165, that ξ is **λ/λ_\*** (coupling strength as a
  fraction of the stability-bound coupling, with conjectured critical ξ_c=0.5), a completely
  different quantity from the Tc/ω_max ratio computed in this task. No conflation was made
  in the script; flagging this here only to preempt confusion when reading the CSV notes
  alongside this table.

**Task C verdict: completed. No document claim was being checked against a fixed target
here (the task is exploratory/descriptive), so there is no "match/mismatch" verdict — the
result is simply that none of the 9 usable record points sit close to the Semenok bounds in
ω_max units, and the Esterlis-vs-Semenok tension is confirmed to be a frequency-convention
artifact, consistent with the document's own §1 discussion.**

Electron-phonon coupling and superconductivity in an alkaline earth hydride CaH6 at high pressures

Abstract

Recently, an alkaline earth hydride CaH6 having a sodalitelike clathrate structure has been experimentally synthesized at megabar pressures with a maximum TcT_{c} of 215 K, comparable to that of a rare earth hydride LaH10. Here, based on first-principles calculations, we find that CaH6 exhibits a huge peak in the Eliashberg spectral function α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F around the low-frequency region of H-derived phonon modes, in contrast to LaH10 having a widely spreading spectrum of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F over the whole frequencies of H-derived phonon modes. It is revealed that the huge peak of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F in CaH6 is associated with an effective electron-phonon coupling (EPC) between low-frequency optical phonons and hybridized H 1ss and Ca 3dd states near the Fermi energy. As pressure increases, the strengthened H−-H covalent bonding not only induces a hardening of optical phonon modes but also reduces the electron-phonon matrix elements related to the low-frequency optical modes, thereby leading to a lowering of the EPC constant. It is thus demonstrated that H-derived low-frequency phonon modes play an important role in the pressure-induced variation of TcT_{c} in CaH6. Furthermore, unlike the presence of two distinct superconducting gaps in LaH10, CaH6 is found to exhibit a single isotropic superconducting gap.

I I. INTRODUCTION

Hydride materials under high pressures have recently attracted great attention in condensed matter physics because of their most plausible candidates for the realization of room-temperature superconductivity (SC) review-Zurek; review-Eremets. In such compressed hydrides, H atoms can be "chemically precompressed" Hydride_Ashcroft to become metallic at relatively lower pressures than the solid phase of elemental hydrogen that requires a high pressure over ∼{\sim}400 GPa MetalicH-Science2017; MetalicH-Nature2020. The SC in metallic hydrogen has been explained by the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) theory, where the superconducting state is represented as a quantum condensate of electron pairs, so-called Cooper pairs, due to electron-phonon interactions BCS. Motivated by the theoretical predictions of high superconducting transition temperature TcT_{c} in compressed hydrides H3S-Sci.Rep2014; H2S-JCP2014, experimentalists successfully synthesized sulfur hydride H3S using diamond anvil cell (DAC) techniques diamondanvil-Rev2009; diamondanvil-Rev2018 and measured TcT_{\rm c} around 203 K at ∼{\sim}155 GPa ExpH3S-Nature2015. This remarkable experimental realization of a higher TcT_{c} record than those of unconventional superconductors such as cuprates Rev-cuprate-2006; Rev-cuprate-2015 and pnictides Rev-pnictide-2011; Rev-pnictide-2015 triggered a large number of theoretical and experimental studies to search for high-TcT_{c} superconductors from compressed hydrides. Most of high-TcT_{c} hydrides are rare earth superhydrides XHn (X = Y ExpYH-Oganov; ExpYH-Eremets; ExpYH-Dias, La ExpLaH10-PRL2019; ExpLaH10-Nature2019; Chongze_PRB_2019; Artur_PRB2020, and Ce ExpCeH9-Nat.Commun2019T.Cui; ExpCeH9-Nat.Commun2019-J.F.Lin; ExpCeH-PRL2021; CeH9-hyunsoo; CeH9-PRB) having H sodalitelike clathrate structures, where each X atom is surrounded by H cage composed of an abnormally large amount of H atoms (nn = 6, 9, and 10). Here, the SC of XHn is driven via strong electron-phonon interaction within the clathrate H networks where the H−-H bond lengths are close to that of solid metallic hydrogen FeH5-Science2017; Shichang-IC. Among existing superhydrides, LaH10 has the highest observed TcT_{c} record of 250∼{\sim}260 K at a pressure of ∼{\sim}170 GPa ExpLaH10-PRL2019; ExpLaH10-Nature2019. Recently, the observation of room-temperature SC was made in carbonaceous sulfur hydride with a TcT_{\rm c} of 288 K at ∼{\sim}267 GPa Exp-CSH-Nature2020. Therefore, the experimental observations of high-temperature SC in surfur-containing hydrides Exp-CSH-Nature2020; ExpH3S-Nature2015 and rare earth superhydrides ExpYH-Oganov; ExpYH-Eremets; ExpYH-Dias; ExpLaH10-PRL2019; ExpLaH10-Nature2019; Chongze_PRB_2019; Artur_PRB2020; ExpCeH9-Nat.Commun2019T.Cui; ExpCeH9-Nat.Commun2019-J.F.Lin; ExpCeH-PRL2021; CeH9-hyunsoo; CeH9-PRB have launched a new era of high-TcT_{\rm c} superconductors.

As another kind of high-TcT_{c} hydride having a H clathrate structure, the alkaline earth metal superhydride CaH6 with the high crystalline symmetry of space group I​m​3¯​mIm\overline{3}m [see Fig. 1(a)] was theoretically predicted to exhibit a TcT_{c} of 235 K at 150 GPa CaH6-PANS2012. To verify this prediction, Yanming Ma and his coworkers CaH6-arXiv2021 have recently synthesized CaH6 by a laser heated DAC technique and measured a maximum TcT_{c} of 215 K at 172 GPa. Such an experimental confirmation of the high TcT_{c} over 200 K in CaH6 extends the realm to search for room-temperature superconductors in non-rare earth metal superhydrides. To understand how high-temperature SC emerges in CaH6, it is necessary to investigate its salient electronic, bonding, and phononic properties from which large electron-phonon coupling (EPC) is derived. Interestingly, despite similar H clathrate structures, CaH6 shows the drastically different characteristics of the Eliashberg spectral function α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F compared to the high-TcT_{c} rare earth hydride LaH10, as discussed below.

In this article, we study the electronic, bonding, and phononic properties of compressed CaH6 using first-principles density functional theory (DFT) and density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) calculations. It is found that Ca 4ss state is nearly unoccupied while Ca 3dd states become occupied below the Fermi energy EFE_{F} to hybridize with H 1ss state, leading to a mixed ionic-covalent bonding between Ca atoms and H cages. This rearrangement of valence electronic states is likely attributed to an increased Coulomb repulsion between Ca atom and its surrounding H atoms at high pressure. Interestingly, in contrast to LaH10 having the widely spreading spectrum of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F over the whole frequencies of H phonon modes, CaH6 exhibits a huge peak in α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F around the low-frequency region of H-derived phonon modes. This peculiar feature of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F in CaH6 can be explained in terms of a more effective EPC between low-frequency optical phonons and H 1ss state hybridized with rather delocalized Ca 3dd states. Meanwhile, for LaH10, a strong hybridization between H 1ss and localized La 4ff states Liangliang-PRB near EFE_{F} is likely to weaken the enhancement of EPC related to H-derived low-frequency phonon modes. We find that, as pressure increases, the mixed ionic-covalent Ca−-H bonding is strengthened to decrease the H−-H bond length, which leads to lowering of the EPC constant via optical phonon hardening as well as the reduction of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F. Therefore, H-derived low-frequency phonon modes in compressed CaH6 are of importance for the variation of TcT_{c} with respect to pressure. We further find that CaH6 has a single isotropic superconducting gap, in contrast to LaH10 having two distinct superconducting gaps chongze-LaH10.

II II. COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

We performed first-principles DFT calculations using the Vienna ab initio simulation package vasp1; vasp2. The potential of the core was described by the projector augmented wave method paw. For the exchange-correlation interaction between the valence electrons, we employed the generalized-gradient approximation functional of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof pbe. The 3s2s^{2}3p6p^{6} semicore electrons of Ca atom were included in the electronic-structure calculations. A plane-wave basis was used with a kinetic energy cutoff of 400 eV. The Brillouin-zone integration was done with the 24×{\times}24×{\times}24 kk-point mesh. All atoms were allowed to relax along the calculated forces until all the residual force components were less than 0.001 eV/Å. The lattice dynamics calculation was carried out by using the QUANTUM ESPRESSO package QE with optimized norm-conserving Vanderbilt pseudopotentials Dojo (see Fig. S1 in the Supplemental Material supple) and a kinetic energy cutoff of 1088 eV. Here, we used the 6×6×66\times 6\times 6 qq-point mesh for the phonon dispersion calculation. For the EPC calculation, we used the software EPW epw-iso; epw-aniso; epw-code with the 24×24×2424\times 24\times 24 qq- and 48×48×4848\times 48\times 48 kk-point meshes in the Brillouin zone.

III III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

We begin by optimizing the structure of compressed CaH6 as a function of pressure using DFT calculations. Based on previous theoretical and experimental studies CaH6-PANS2012; CaH6-arXiv2021, we consider the I​m​3¯​mIm\overline{3}m phase of CaH6 where each Ca atom in the bcc lattice is surrounded by H cage composed of twenty four H atoms [see Fig. 1(a)]. Figure 1(b) shows that the calculated lattice constants of compressed CaH6 decrease monotonously with increasing pressure. Accordingly, the size of H cage is reduced with increasing pressure, therefore decreasing the H−-H and Ca−-H bond lengths (denoted as dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} and dCa−Hd_{\rm Ca-H}, respectively), as shown in Fig. 1(b). These changes of dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} and dCa−Hd_{\rm Ca-H} can be intimately linked to the variation in the H−-H and Ca−-H covalent bonding strengths as a function of pressure. Figures 2(a), 2(b), and 2(c) display the charge densities ρ{\rho} of CaH6 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively. Here, each H-H bond has a saddle point of charge density at its midpoint. The calculated ρ{\rho} values at the midpoints of the H−-H bond are 0.60, 0.65, and 0.70 ee/Å3 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively, indicating that the H−-H covalent bonding strength increases as pressure increases. Similarly, the covalent character of the Ca−-H bond is strengthened with increasing pressure, because the ρ{\rho} values at the saddle points [marked ×{\times} in Figs. 2(a), 2(b), and 2(c)] between Ca and H atoms are 0.33, 0.37, and 0.40 ee/Å3 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively. Such pressure-dependent bond lengths and the corresponding covalent bonding strengths in compressed CaH6 affect H-derived phonon frequencies and EPC with respect to pressure, as discussed below.

Figure 3(a) shows the comparison of the electronic band structures of compressed CaH6 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa. We find that the bands below (above) EFE_{F} shift downward (upward) with increasing pressure, reflecting a band widening due to reduced lattice constants. It is, however, noticeable that the band dispersions around EFE_{F} change very little with respect to pressure. Consequently, the density of states (DOS) near EFE_{F} is insensitive to a change in pressure. Such a nearly invariance of electronic states near EFE_{F} implies that the pressure-induced variations of EPC and TcT_{c} are mostly driven by phonon effect, as discussed below. In order to explore the hybridization between Ca and H electronic states, we present the partial DOS (PDOS), obtained at 150 GPa, in Fig. 3(b). We find that the occupied states around EFE_{F} are dominantly composed of H 1ss and Ca 3dd states with a minor component of Ca 4ss state. Under high pressure, Ca 3dd states that are the excited states in Ca atom become occupied below EFE_{F}, while Ca 4ss state residing further away from the atomic nucleus is pulled above EFE_{F} due to an increased Coulomb repulsion between Ca and H atoms. Especially, there are large PDOS peaks of Ca 3pp states around −-10 and −-22 eV [see Fig. 3(b)], indicating a large delocalization of semicore electrons at high pressure. It is also seen in Fig. 3(b) that (i) the PDOS patterns of Ca 3pp and 3dd states closely resemble that of H 1ss state and (ii) H 1ss state is absent in the energy range between −-17 and −-21 eV where the gap of Ca-derived states exist. These results indicate a hybridization between H- and Ca-derived states. In Fig. 3(c), we find that the numbers of electrons occupying Ca 3pp and 3dd states between −-17 eV and EFE_{F} increase with increasing pressure, whereas that occupying Ca 3ss state is negligible. This implies that the covalent character of Ca−-H bond with the hybridization of H 1ss and Ca 3pp/3dd states is strengthened with increasing pressure. Meanwhile, our Bader analysis Bader shows that the Bader charges of Ca atom increase as 0.99, 1.07, and 1.14ee at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively (see Fig. S2 in the Supplemental Material supple), indicating that the ionic character of Ca−-H bond due to an electron transfer from Ca to H atoms is weakened with increasing pressure. Based on the results of electronic states and Bader analysis, we can say that the Ca−-H bond has a mixed ionic-covalent bonding character, similar to the La−-H bond in LaH10 Seho-PRM.

Next, we examine the phonon spectrum of CaH6 as a function of pressure using DFPT calculations QE. Figures 4(a), 4(b), and 4(c) dispaly the calculated phonon dispersions at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively, together with their projected DOS onto Ca and H atoms. We find that (i) the acoustic phonon modes of Ca atoms are well separated from the optical phonon modes of H atoms and (ii) as pressure increases, H-derived phonon modes shift upwards in the overall frequency range. The logarithmic average ωlog{\omega}_{\rm log} of phonon frequencies is estimated to increase as 684, 851, and 933 cm-1 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively. Such pressure-induced phonon hardening can be attributed to the strengthened H−-H covalent bonding character with increasing pressure. It is noted that at 120 GPa, H-derived low-frequency modes shift towards Ca-derived acoustic modes [see Fig. S3(a) in the Supplemental Material supple]. Such a softening of H-derived low-frequency modes is possibly due to an increase in dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} as pressure decreases, giving rise to a suppression of restoring forces between H atoms: i.e., increased dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} gives rise to an effective screening of low-frequency optical modes with the hybridized H 1ss and Ca 3dd states near EFE_{F}, as discussed below. At a pressure below 120 GPa, there appear imaginary phonon modes along the H−NH-N line [see Fig. S3(b)], indicating that the I​m​3¯​mIm\overline{3}m phase of CaH6 becomes unstable. It is thus likely that the phononic properties and dynamical instability of compressed CaH6 are intimately correlated with the covalent bonding strength in the Ca-encapsulated H-cage structure via dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} and dCa−Hd_{\rm Ca-H}.

Using the isotropic Migdal-Eliashberg formalism Migdal; Eliash; ME-review, we calculate α2​F​(ω){\alpha}^{2}F({\omega}) and integrated EPC constant λ⁡(ω){\lambda}({\omega}). Figures 4(a), 4(b), and 4(c) include the results of α2​F​(ω){\alpha}^{2}F({\omega}) and λ⁡(ω){\lambda}({\omega}), calculated at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively. We find that α2​F​(ω){\alpha}^{2}F({\omega}) exhibits a huge peak around the low-frequency region of H-derived phonon modes, which in turn yields a steep increase of λ⁡(ω){\lambda}({\omega}). At 150 GPa, the phonon modes between 500 and 1200 cm-1 are estimated to contribute to ∼{\sim}78% of the total EPC constant λ{\lambda} = λ{\lambda}(∞{\infty}), while Ca-derived phonon modes reach ∼{\sim}14%. As illustrated by red circles in Figs. 4(a), 4(b), and 4(c), the EPC strength of H-derived low-frequency modes decreases with increasing pressure. We find that λ{\lambda} values decrease as 2.75, 1.99, and 1.71 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively. Note that λ{\lambda} has a maximum value of 4.08 at 120 GPa. Such a lowering of λ{\lambda} under pressure can be explained in terms of the hardening of optical phonon modes as well as the reduction of the electron-phonon matrix elements [g𝐪​νg_{{\bf q}\nu} in Eq. (2)] related to low-frequency optical phonon modes.

It is remarkable that the pattern of α2​F​(ω){\alpha}^{2}F({\omega}) in CaH6 contrasts strikingly with that of LaH10 which has an even spread over most frequencies of H phonon modes [see Fig. 4(d)]. As a result, for LaH10, λ⁡(ω){\lambda}({\omega}) computed at 250 GPa increases monotonously as ω{\omega} increases up to ∼{\sim}1800 cm-1, indicating that most of phonon modes contribute to EPC. These contrasting features of α2​F​(ω){\alpha}^{2}F({\omega}) and λ⁡(ω){\lambda}({\omega}) between CaH6 and LaH10 may be attributed to the different aspects of their electronic and bonding properties: i.e., (i) for CaH6, H 1ss state hybridizes with rather delocalized Ca 3dd states near EFE_{F}, in comparison with LaH10 where H 1ss state hybridizes with localized La 4ff states Liangliang-PRB and (ii) CaH6 has a longer dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} than LaH10 (see Fig. S4 in the Supplemental Material supple). These aspects of CaH6 can be linked to a more dominant contribution of H-derived low-frequency modes to α2​F​(ω){\alpha}^{2}F({\omega}): i.e., the longer dH−Hd_{\rm H-H} in CaH6 likely gives rise to larger electron-phonon matrix elements between low-frequency optical phonon modes and hybridized H 1ss and Ca 3dd states near EFE_{F}, compared to the case of LaH10.

We further study the anisotropy of superconducting gap Δ{\Delta} using the anisotropic Migdal-Eliashberg formalism Migdal; Eliash; ME-review. Figure S5 in the Supplemental Material supple shows the distribution of 𝐤{\bf k}-resolved EPC constant λn​𝐤{\lambda}_{n{\bf k}}, obtained at 150 GPa. Here, nn represents the band index and all available electron-phonon scattering processes connecting the electronic states between 𝐤{\bf k} and other 𝐤{\bf k} points are included. With a typical Coulomb pseudopotential parameter of μ∗{\mu}^{*} = 0.13 in superhydrides CaH6-PANS2012; rare-earth-hydride-PANS2017; rare-earth-hydride-PRL2017, we calculate the temperature dependence of Δ{\Delta} at 150 GPa. Figure 5(a) displays the energy distribution of Δ{\Delta} as a function of temperature, indicating that there is a single nodeless superconducting gap. Here, Δ{\Delta} closes at TcT_{\rm c} ≈{\approx} 240 K, which is almost the same as that (238 K) obtained from the isotropic Migdal-Eliashberg formalism [see the dashed line in Fig. 5(a)]. For TT << 100 K, Δ{\Delta} is distributed in the range of 48−-58 meV, which is correlated with the distribution of λn​𝐤{\lambda}_{n{\bf k}} (see Fig. S5 in the Supplemental Material supple). Figure 5(b) shows the nn- and 𝐤{\bf k}-resolved superconducting gap Δn​𝐤{\Delta}_{n{\bf k}} on the three Fermi surface sheets arising from the nn = 1, 2, and 3 bands, displaying rather uniform superconducting gaps. It is thus demonstrated that CaH6 exhibits a single isotropic superconducting gap, in contrast to LaH10 with two distinct superconducting gaps chongze-LaH10.

As pressure increases, TcT_{c} values estimated using the isotropic Migdal-Eliashberg formalism decrease as 238, 220, and 210 K at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively (see Fig. S6 in the Supplemental Material supple). This lowering of TcT_{c} with increasing pressure is determined mainly by a decrease of λ{\lambda}, despite an increase of ωlog{\omega}_{\rm log} [see Figs. 4(a), 4(b), and 4(c)]. Recently, Belli e​tet a​lal. networking proposed the correlation between electronic bonding network and TcT_{c} in superconducting hydrides, where the networking value ϕ{\phi} is defined as the highest value of the ELF that creates an isosurface spanning through the whole crystal in all three Cartesian directions. It was claimed networking that the larger the value of ϕ{\phi}, the higher is TcT_{c}. In order to examine the ϕ{\phi} vs TcT_{c} relation, we plot the relevant ELF isosurfaces at 150, 200, and 250 GPa in Figs. 2(d), 2(e), and 2(f), resepctively. Here, ϕ{\phi} values are estimated to be 0.624, 0.626, and 0.628 at 150, 200, and 250 GPa, respectively. This increase of ϕ{\phi} with increasing pressure is consistent with an increase in ρ{\rho} at the midpoint of H−-H bond [see Figs. 2(a), 2(b), and 2(c)]. Therefore, we can say that for CaH6, ϕ{\phi} increases as pressure increases, but TcT_{c} decreases with strengthened H-H bonds at higher pressure.

IV IV. CONCLUSION

Using first-principles calculations, we have investigated the EPC and SC in an alkaline earth hydride CaH6 at high pressures. Unlike the high-TcT_{c} rare earth hydride LaH10 having a widespread of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F over the whole frequencies of H-derived phonon modes, CaH6 exhibits a huge peak in α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F around the low-frequency region of H-derived phonon modes. We revealed that such a drastically different feature of the Eliashberg spectral function in CaH6 is associated with an effective EPC between low-frequency optical phonons and H 1ss state hybridized with Ca 3dd states. Furthermore, as pressure increases, the covalent H−-H bond is strengthened to yield optical phonon hardening as well as the reduction of α2​F{\alpha}^{2}F, resulting in a lowering of λ{\lambda}. It was thus demonstrated that H-derived low-frequency phonon modes play an important role in the pressure-dependent variation of TcT_{c} in compressed CaH6. The present findings have important implications for understanding the EPC and SC in alkaline earth superhydrides, which can shed light on the discovery of room-temperature superconductors in non-rare earth metal superhydrides.
